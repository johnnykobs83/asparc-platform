The ASPARC
Warm-Up

One warm-up structure for every team, from juniors to seniors. It gets players ready to perform, cuts non-contact injuries, and switches their heads on before the first whistle. The core is Football Australia's Perform+ running block; the rest is built around what the session or match needs.

Why we do it

Three jobs, every time

Perform

A structured warm-up with light running, dynamic movement and football-specific work measurably improves sprinting, jumping and kicking. Most of the benefit comes from warmer muscles and more blood flow to the working muscles.

Protect

The warm-up is our most reliable slot for injury prevention: strength, control, landing and change-of-direction work. Done consistently across a season it reduces strains, sprains and other non-contact injuries.

Prepare

Touches, passing and possession in the warm-up settle players on the ball and lock their attention onto the session. Use the possession game to rehearse the tactical idea of the day.

Adjust to the day

Hot days

Shorten phases 2 and 4, keep the Perform+ block, and add drink breaks. Follow your club's heat policy and Football Australia heat guidance.

Cold days

Extend the running block by a few minutes and keep players moving between phases. Bibs and drills set up before players arrive.

Individual needs

Players coming back from injury do their prescribed activation with the physio first, then join in. Flag anyone on a return-to-play plan before the session starts.

Growing players

For U13–U15s in a growth spurt, watch for knee or heel pain and cut bounding and plyometric volume if it appears. Quality over quantity.

The session ahead

A high-speed session needs a full phase 4. A recovery or tactical walk-through session can keep phase 4 short.

Half-time

Players cool quickly in the break. Run 2–3 minutes of re-warm before the second half: strides, quick feet and a few short accelerations.

For coaches

Coaching standards

  • Every team runs the Perform+ running block at every training session and every match. No exceptions for time.
  • Phases always run in order: raise, mobilise, ball work, then speed. Kick-off or the main session starts within 5 minutes of phase 4 ending.
  • The Perform+ Performance exercises (strength, balance and plyometrics) are done at least twice a week, during training, at the end of the session or at home in about 10 minutes.
  • Coaches watch technique, not just completion. Knees in line with hips and ankles on every landing and cut.
  • Report any player who can't complete the warm-up pain-free to the physio or medical staff the same day.
